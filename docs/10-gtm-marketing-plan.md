# Go-To-Market & Marketing Plan

Goal per the founder's brief: real users, real revenue — not vanity metrics. Every channel below is chosen for being realistic for a solo, bootstrapped founder, sourced from actual GTM research rather than generic startup advice.

## Channel 1: Dogfooding / content-led SEO (the highest-leverage, slowest-to-start channel)

Seovate's own marketing site should be the first customer of Seovate. If the product can't get its own site ranking for terms like "autonomous SEO agent for small business" or "AI SEO tool for local business," it has no credibility pitching the same capability to strangers. This is simultaneously the cheapest channel available and the best proof-of-product demo.

- Practical note from research: content/SEO-led CAC runs roughly $341–$533/customer with a 6–12 month ramp — this is not a fast channel, but it compounds (unlike paid ads) and should start on day one of the roadmap, not after launch.
- Publish real build-in-public content (founder's own journey building an autonomous SEO tool) as a secondary content angle — this doubles as Indie Hackers / community content (Channel 2) with near-zero extra effort.

## Channel 2: Founder-led community presence (fast to start, direct feedback loop)

Sourced-viable communities for this exact audience: Indie Hackers, r/SEO, r/startups, r/SaaS, r/smallbusiness, r/marketing, r/Productivity, r/localseo. Precedent: Ahrefs' early growth is commonly attributed to founders answering real questions in forums before ever selling — trust-first, not pitch-first.

- **Tactic:** answer real SEO questions on r/SEO and r/smallbusiness for several weeks before ever mentioning the product by name (per community norms — these communities are notoriously hostile to drive-by self-promotion). Only mention Seovate when directly relevant and disclosed as the founder's product.
- **Tactic:** an Indie Hackers build-in-public log documenting real revenue/customer numbers — this audience specifically rewards transparency about numbers, which also forces useful founder discipline (see roadmap's revenue milestones).

## Channel 3: Cold outreach to local businesses (direct, scrappy, matches the ICP)

Sourced benchmarks: generic cold email reply rates run 1–5%; smaller companies (the ICP here) reply more than enterprise; highly personalized outreach can reach 15–25%.

- **Tactic:** hyper-targeted, small-batch outreach to the specific verticals named in [02-icp-positioning.md](02-icp-positioning.md) (local dentists, contractors, etc. — pick 1–2 verticals to start, not all of them), personalized with a real, specific finding about their actual current website ("your site is missing meta descriptions on 12 pages, here's what that's costing you") generated by literally running Seovate's own audit pipeline on their site before ever emailing them. This is both more effective outreach and a direct product demo baked into the pitch.
- Do not scale this to mass/generic cold email — the data explicitly shows personalization is the entire difference between a 2% and a 20% reply rate, and generic blasts also carry real deliverability/reputation risk for the sending domain, which matters long-term for a company whose product is about email/SEO reputation.

## Channel 4: Agency & web-dev-shop partnerships (distribution multiplier, not the direct ICP)

Sourced finding: white-label SEO reseller models (agencies reselling automated services under their own brand) are an established, real structure. Local web-design agencies, freelance WordPress developers, and small marketing consultancies already have the exact SMB relationships Seovate wants — but not the SEO automation capability.

- **Tactic (post-MVP, not week 1):** once the direct-to-SMB product is proven (per the MVP success criteria), approach a small number of local web-dev shops/freelancers with a simple referral or white-label arrangement — they get a value-add for their existing clients, Seovate gets distribution without direct SMB acquisition cost. This is explicitly deferred past MVP because it requires product surfaces (multi-client visibility, white-labeling) that are out of scope for v1 (see [03-mvp-scope.md](03-mvp-scope.md)) — don't build agency features before there's a direct-sale product worth reselling.

## Channel 5: Product Hunt launch (one-time spike, useful for initial signal and backlinks)

Sourced findings: Product Hunt typically converts 10–30 signups per 1,000 visitors; outcomes are highly variable and depend more on audience fit than upvote count (one founder's smaller, well-targeted launch converted 91 paying customers from 300 upvotes; a bigger, less-targeted launch converted just 1 customer from 612 upvotes).

- **Tactic:** launch only once there's a working, demonstrable product with a fast "wow" moment (the technical-audit trial experience from [09-pricing-monetization.md](09-pricing-monetization.md)) — a Product Hunt audience is unforgiving of vaporware, and this is a one-shot-per-product channel worth using well, not early.

## Explicitly deprioritized for MVP

- **Paid ads (Google/Meta):** not sourced as a strong fit for this stage — CAC via paid channels is typically higher and doesn't compound the way content/SEO does, and the founder's own budget constraint (<$500/mo) makes meaningful paid spend impractical anyway.
- **AppSumo/LTD launch:** deferred, per the reasoning in [09-pricing-monetization.md](09-pricing-monetization.md) — real risk to reliability reputation if scaled too fast before the guardrail system is proven.

## The 90-day GTM sequence, concretely

See [11-roadmap-90day.md](11-roadmap-90day.md) for how this integrates with the build timeline — in short: community presence and dogfooding content start immediately (parallel to building), personalized cold outreach begins the moment there's a working audit pipeline (even before full autonomous publishing is live, since the audit alone is a sellable, demonstrable wedge), and Product Hunt/partnerships come after the MVP success criteria are met, not before.
